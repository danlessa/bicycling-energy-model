## 2. The open question I would not skip

**τ came out region-dependent on the full grid: 6 m on D3–D5, 2 m on D6.**

The two-level pass had 4 m for both, and §3.1.5 currently asserts "one law, one
form, one deadband, two constants". That sentence is not safe as written.

Two readings, and the raw dump can distinguish them without re-simulating:

- **Real.** Sustained Alpine descents carry less sub-scale elevation noise, so
  they want less filtering.
- **Artefact.** Entry 56 measured τ's optimum as extremely flat (0.2% loss
  inflation at ±10%, and 4–8 m spanning under 2.5%), so the grid may simply be
  resolving noise near a plateau.

To check: `e61_raw.full.csv` carries F3's `E0`/`E1` at **every** τ in
`TAU_SWEEP` for all 145,800 route-combinations. Plot the loss-vs-τ curve per
region and see whether there are two distinct minima or one broad basin. This is
arithmetic on the CSV — no canonical runs needed.

## 4. Traps this session fell into

All three are now in `CLAUDE.md`'s invariants, but the stories are worth having.

**A held parameter can invert a conclusion.** Three instances, each in code
already producing published numbers:

- `e54_transfer` fitted at `TAU_PUB_I` (2 m) while the selected form used 6 m.
  This inflated every per-rider optimum, made the pooled constant look badly
  placed, produced a *negative* transfer penalty, and manufactured a convincing
  case for rider-weighted pooling that evaporated on inspection (Entry 59).
- `e61_sweep` pinned F4's `c` at the published 3.0 m/km. F4's regional gap came
  out negative and F3's was understated, which supported a "the split is
  F3-specific, suspect the deadband" reading that was simply wrong.
- `e54`'s pooled comparator was the literal `0.2879`, stale the moment Entry 55
  changed the aero estimator.

**The fix that generalises:** when a harness needs a fitted constant, read it
from the producing CSV. `e54_transfer._pooled_eps()` is the pattern.

**Never memoise on `id()`.** The sensitivity analyses build throwaway per-ride
dicts with one perturbed component; CPython recycles their ids and a stale entry
answers for a different row. Symptom: every breaking point in `e58_intervals`
read exactly `1.00×`.

**A loss must not change its population with its parameter.** `logratio` skipped
rides whose predicted energy went non-positive; since E(ε) falls with ε, the
optimiser was partly rewarded for making badly-fitting rides vanish.

**And one about flags.** `E61_FULL=1` originally left `E61_COMBOS` at its default
64, so the first "full grid" run swept 64 of 729, finished in 23 minutes, and
looked complete. A flag that quietly does a fraction of what it says is worse
than one that errors.
